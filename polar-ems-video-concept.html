Master style prompt

Paste this once as a style prefix (or system/reference prompt, depending on your tool) before every individual shot prompt in Section 5. It's what keeps 10 separately generated clips looking like one film.

A premium, cinematic documentary about an Antarctic polar research station, shot in the visual language of a high-end technology product launch fused with nature-documentary realism. Consistent color grade: deep cold blues, whites and greys with warm amber interior-light accents and occasional aurora green/violet in the sky. Environment: vast snow and ice fields, a small isolated research station with wind turbines, solar panels, satellite dishes, and frost-covered metal structures; realistic blowing snow and atmospheric haze. Camera language: slow, confident, smooth movement — gentle drone pushes, controlled dolly-ins, restrained handheld only for brief tension beats; no whip pans, no shaky found-footage look. Lighting: natural polar light — blue-hour twilight, overcast diffusion, warm practical window light, aurora as the only stylized light source. Technology reads as realistic and grounded: physical hardware (turbines, panels, sensors, cables, monitors), not glowing holograms; any on-screen digital data is restrained, thin-lined, teal-and-white, resembling real telemetry rather than sci-fi HUDs. Mood: quiet tension resolving into confidence and calm competence. Photorealistic, 4K, shallow depth of field where appropriate, consistent lens character (35–85mm equivalent), subtle anamorphic flare only on wide exterior shots. No on-screen text baked into the video — text is composited in post.
Negative / avoid: cartoon, illustration, anime, oversaturated neon, cyberpunk, glowing holograms, futuristic sci-fi HUD overlays, unrealistic robots, warped hands, distorted or close-up human faces, baked-in text or watermarks, blurry or low-resolution output, extra limbs, morphing geometry.

Faces are deliberately avoided throughout — current AI video tools render them inconsistently. Use silhouettes, gloved hands, or wide figures in parkas wherever a human presence helps a shot.

Incorporating your real dashboard footage

Five inserts, all from polar-ems-web.vercel.app. None of this needs AI video generation — it's a straightforward screen recording composited into the AI-generated monitor shots.

1
Record at native resolution, full-screen
1920×1080 minimum (2560×1440 or higher preferred). Hide the browser chrome — use F11 fullscreen or crop it in the edit.
2
Trigger motion before you hit record
Open the Demo Scenarios panel and pick a scenario relevant to that scene (e.g. "Low renewable generation" for Scene 6, "Internet outage" for Scene 7) a few seconds before recording, so charts and badges are actively animating rather than sitting static.
3
Capture each tab separately, 6–10s per clip
Dashboard, Forecast, Optimizer, Anomalies & Maintenance, Offline Autonomy, Green Audit — six short clips covering the five insert points (Offline Autonomy needs two beats: the alert, then the OFFLINE badge).
4
Record at 60fps if your tool allows it
The charts and the flow-diagram's dashed-line animation read far more smoothly at 60fps than 30fps when composited into a cinematic shot.
5
Composite as a screen-insert, not a flat overlay
In your editor (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere, or CapCut), corner-pin or perspective-warp the recording onto the AI-generated monitor's screen area; add a faint screen glow/bloom and a very subtle reflection to sell it as physically on that display, matching the site's own dark teal palette so it never looks pasted on.
6
Add gentle motion even to a static capture
A slow 3–5% Ken Burns zoom on every screen insert prevents it from reading as a flat, dead screenshot.
7
Hide or restyle the cursor
A default OS cursor breaks the premium feel. Hide it during recording (most screen recorders have this option), or replace clicks with a subtle animated highlight ring added in post.
