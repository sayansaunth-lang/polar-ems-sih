Forecast-aware dispatch for isolated, diesel-dependent microgrids

A working prototype, built for an Antarctic research station, that measurably cuts diesel consumption and generator wear on any remote power system where fuel is expensive to deliver and downtime is not an option.

01Executive Summary

POLAR-EMS is a dispatch and safety layer for isolated microgrids — systems that generate their own power from wind, solar, battery and diesel with no utility grid to fall back on. It replaces fixed-rule generator scheduling with a forecast-aware controller that decides, continuously, how to blend available power sources to minimize diesel burned and generator runtime, without ever compromising a hard-guaranteed power floor for critical loads.

Over an extended validation run of the working prototype, the AI-optimized controller reduced diesel consumption and generator operating hours relative to an equally realistic fixed-rule baseline, while a safety layer architecturally independent of the optimizer guaranteed critical-load power throughout — including under simulated AI failure and simulated connectivity loss.

02The Industry Problem

This is not a problem unique to polar research. Any facility that generates its own power in a location fuel cannot easily reach shares the same two constraints:

  • Diesel is the most expensive input, and it's fixed-schedule delivered. Fuel is shipped, flown, or trucked in on a schedule that cannot flex with demand — every litre burned inefficiently is a cost that compounds over the life of the site.
  • Downtime is not cosmetic. For remote telecom, defense, mining, medical, or life-support infrastructure, a power failure is an operational or safety incident, not an inconvenience — so any optimization layer has to prove it cannot cause one.

Most existing remote-site generator controllers are fixed-threshold rule systems: run diesel when battery crosses X%, regardless of what the weather forecast says is coming. That leaves real, measurable efficiency on the table.

03How It Works

Three layers, each doing one job:

  • Forecast-aware dispatch. Instead of reacting only to the current battery level, the controller looks ahead at the renewable forecast and times diesel starts and battery reserve to avoid unnecessary generator cycling.
  • A hard safety floor, independent of the optimizer. A separate override guarantees critical-load power regardless of what the optimizer decides, or whether it's running at all — demonstrated by disabling the AI controller entirely and confirming the site stays powered on deterministic fallback rules.
  • Machine learning that's real, not asserted. Load forecasting is backed by a gradient-boosting model trained on a genuine held-out test split, exported and verified to reproduce its training-time predictions exactly, then deployed to run inference directly in the field-facing application with no cloud dependency.

07Maturity & Path to Deployment

Presented at the stage it actually is: a validated software prototype, not yet integrated with physical hardware.

The simulation, dispatch, forecasting and safety logic are built against the same data shape real sensors and a real battery-management system would provide (weather, state-of-charge, generator status, per-zone load), and both the client and server implementations already expose that logic as a real, callable API. Moving to a physical deployment means replacing the synthetic telemetry generator with live sensor/BMS/genset feeds into the same pipeline — not a redesign of the decision logic itself.

What's already real today: the dispatch algorithm, the safety override, the trained forecasting models, and a live, publicly deployed frontend and backend that anyone can query over HTTPS right now.
